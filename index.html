About

I am a second-year Ph.D. candidate at NAIST, where I apply deep learning to large-scale healthcare data and build knowledge graphs for sparse biological databases. My doctoral research focuses on predicting antimicrobial resistance from electronic health records using graph neural networks.

Before my doctoral studies, I spent 20 years in data management and analytics for international development programs in Bangladesh, working with USAID activities, FHI 360, and WorldFish. Much of that work dealt with one problem, making data reported by many organizations trustworthy and then making it legible to decision-makers. That background shapes my research today, with a strong interest in data quality, leakage-aware evaluation, and fairness in medical AI.

Research

Antimicrobial resistance prediction from EHRs

Doctoral research using graph neural networks on large-scale clinical records, covering data preprocessing, feature engineering, and rigorous model evaluation. A journal manuscript is under review at IEEE Access.

LLM-assisted knowledge graph construction and enrichment

Building and enriching knowledge graphs with large language models, including work on a sparse plant secondary metabolite database. Developed in part during an Erasmus+ ICM mobility at the University of Trento, Italy (April to July 2026).

Trustworthy evaluation of medical AI

Leakage-aware re-evaluation, operating-point fairness, and cross-dataset generalization of deep learning models for retinal disease and glaucoma screening.

Multi-omics and drug response

Pathway-aware ensemble models for predicting drug response in cancer cell lines.
